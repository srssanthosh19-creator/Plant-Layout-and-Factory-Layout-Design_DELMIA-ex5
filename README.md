PROCESS PLANNING AND SIMULATION OF A SOAP MANUFACTURING PLANT USING DELMIA


1. AIM

To develop the process plan and simulate the plant layout for the batch/mass production of bath soap using industrial processing and material-handling equipment, and to create a virtual manufacturing environment using DELMIA for studying material flow, machine arrangement, material handling, storage, productivity, and workplace safety.


2. OBJECTIVES

1. To study the manufacturing process of bath soap.
2. To identify suitable raw materials and manufacturing operations.
3. To prepare a suitable process sequence for soap production.
4. To identify the machines, equipment, storage areas, and inspection facilities required.
5. To estimate the approximate processing time for the manufacturing operations.
6. To simulate the plant layout using DELMIA 3DEXPERIENCE Plant Layout Design.
7. To study the material flow between different processing stations.
8. To minimize unnecessary material movement and handling.
9. To incorporate storage areas, operator areas, conveyors, and safety fencing in the virtual plant.
10. To visualize the complete soap manufacturing facility in a 3D simulation environment.


3. INTRODUCTION

Soap is a widely used personal-care product manufactured through a series of chemical processing, mixing, refining, forming, cutting, stamping, inspection, and packaging operations.

In this work, a virtual manufacturing plant for bath soap production is considered. The manufacturing system consists of raw-material receiving and storage, mixing, processing/saponification, drying or conditioning, refining, plodding, cutting, stamping, inspection, packaging, and finished-goods storage.

The plant layout is simulated using DELMIA 3DEXPERIENCE Plant Layout Design. The simulation includes processing equipment, conveyors, storage racks, workers, inspection areas, loading and unloading areas, and safety fencing.

The DELMIA simulation provides a 3D virtual representation of the manufacturing facility and helps in visualizing the arrangement of equipment, material flow, storage areas, worker movement, and material-handling systems before physical implementation.

7. MANUFACTURING OPERATIONS


7.1 RAW MATERIAL RECEIVING

Vegetable oils, chemicals, fragrances, colours, additives, and packaging materials are received from suppliers.

The incoming materials are checked and transferred to their respective storage areas.


7.2 RAW MATERIAL STORAGE

The received materials are stored in designated tanks, containers, storage racks, and shelving systems according to their storage requirements.

Proper storage helps maintain material quality and provides easy access during production.


7.3 MIXING / SOAP PREPARATION

The required raw materials are measured according to the production requirements and transferred to the mixing equipment.

The materials are mixed to obtain a uniform soap mixture suitable for further processing.

Machine Used: Industrial Mixing Machine


7.4 SAPONIFICATION / PROCESSING

The oils or fats react with the alkaline material to produce soap.

The process is controlled to obtain the required consistency and properties of the soap material.

Machine Used: Soap Processing / Saponification Unit


7.5 DRYING / CONDITIONING

The soap material is conditioned and excess moisture is reduced to obtain the required consistency for subsequent processing.

Equipment Used: Drying / Conditioning Equipment


7.6 REFINING

The processed soap is passed through a refining operation to obtain a uniform and smooth material.

The refining process improves the consistency and surface quality of the soap material.

Machine Used: Soap Refining Machine


7.7 PLODDING / EXTRUSION

The refined soap is compressed and extruded through a plodder to produce a continuous soap billet of suitable cross-section.

Machine Used: Soap Plodder / Extruder


7.8 CUTTING

The continuous soap billet is cut into individual soap bars of the required size and length.

Machine Used: Soap Cutting Machine


7.9 STAMPING

The required brand name, logo, or product information is stamped onto the soap bar.

Machine Used: Soap Stamping Machine


7.10 INSPECTION

The soap bars are inspected for size, shape, weight, appearance, surface quality, and other required characteristics.

Defective products are separated for rework or rejection.


7.11 PACKAGING

The accepted soap bars are transferred to the packaging station.

The soap bars are wrapped and packed into suitable boxes or cartons.

Machine Used: Packaging Machine


7.12 FINISHED GOODS STORAGE

The packaged soap products are transferred to the finished-goods storage area before dispatch.

The products are arranged in designated storage racks or containers for organized inventory management.

The raw materials are stored in tanks, containers, racks, and shelving systems according to their storage requirements.

The storage area is positioned near the incoming material section to reduce unnecessary transportation.


12.3 PROCESSING AREA

The main soap-processing equipment is positioned inside the production area.

The machines are arranged according to the manufacturing sequence to provide a logical material flow through the virtual plant.


12.4 CONVEYOR SYSTEM

Conveyors are incorporated into the DELMIA simulation to represent the movement of soap material and finished products between different workstations.

The conveyor system helps reduce manual material handling and provides an organized production flow.

The refining machine and soap plodder are positioned after the primary processing section.

12.6 CUTTING AND STAMPING AREA

The soap billet is transferred to the cutting and stamping section.

The cutting machine produces individual soap bars, followed by stamping of the required brand name or product information.


12.7 INSPECTION AREA

An inspection table/workstation is provided for checking the finished soap bars.

The inspection area is positioned before the packaging and finished-goods storage sections.


12.8 PACKAGING AREA

The packaging area is positioned after inspection.

Accepted soap bars are wrapped and packed into suitable boxes or cartons before being transferred to finished-goods storage.


12.9 FINISHED GOODS STORAGE

The finished and packaged soap products are stored in designated racks or storage areas before dispatch.


12.10 LOADING AND UNLOADING AREA

The DELMIA simulation includes truck access at the plant entrance and exit.

The loading and unloading area allows raw materials and finished products to be transported efficiently.


12.11 SAFETY FENCING

Safety fencing is incorporated around the production area to protect workers from machinery and moving equipment and to control access to the manufacturing zone.
